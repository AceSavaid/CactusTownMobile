Cactus Town · home screen

Three ways home could feel

The hub is where a session starts and ends. Right now it's a flat green field with a button column — functional, but it doesn't feel like anywhere. These three directions all aim for the same thing: a calm, lived-in room you're glad to come back to.

The brief
Relaxed and homey. Somewhere your plant lives, not a menu.
Keep
Landscape, flat vector art, the warm desert palette, your customised cactus centre stage.
Menu today
Customise · Mini-Games · Plant Stats · Notices · Town · Settings, plus Sage's tip and two currencies.
The move
Turn that list into things in a scene — and let time of day (EST clock) quietly shift the light.
1

The Windowsill

Build lift · high

References: Assemble with Care, Alto's Odyssey menu, Kimono Cats, Monument Valley chapter select.

The idea

A warm room with a window. Your plant and Sage share the sill; through the glass, the town sits in golden light that tracks the real EST clock. You're not looking at a menu — you're home, looking out.

Menu becomes furniture

  • Door / window → go to Town
  • Shelf of game boxes → Mini-Games
  • Hand mirror → Customise Plant
  • Open journal → Plant Stats & Almanac
  • Corkboard → Notices (a pinned note when a daily's ready)
  • Radio → Settings

Why it's the boldest

Every object needs art in a few lit states, plus a day/night pass. It's the one that would make Cactus Town screenshot like a premium cosy game — and the one that eats the most time.

2

The Potting Bench

Build lift · medium

References: Stardew Valley farmhouse, Animal Crossing: Pocket Camp, Spiritfarer cabin, Cozy Grove.

The idea

Step outside. A potting bench on a porch: hanging plants, string lights that warm up at dusk, terracotta pots along the rail. The town is a short path away. It reads as the small friendly place the game is about.

Menu becomes signage

  • Hand-painted wooden signs flank the bench — Town, Mini-Games, Customise, Plant Stats
  • A little notice board propped on the bench → Notices
  • Sage in a chair at the end of the bench, tip on tap
  • Settings tucked as a lantern or hook, top corner

Why it's the middle path

One scene, mostly reusable prop art you already have (pots, plants, signs, fences). The signs animate on a gentle sway; the lights and a firefly or two carry the "relaxed" without a full lighting system.

3

Cosy Flat

Build lift · low

References: Alba: A Wildlife Adventure UI, A Monster's Expedition, Prune, Wilmot's Warehouse.

The idea

Keep the exact art language — just stop it being flat and empty. A soft sky gradient, layered silhouettes for depth, a wooden deck the plant stands on with a long low-sun shadow. The plant sways; a leaf drifts past now and then.

Menu becomes plaques

  • Rounded wooden plaque buttons, each with a small icon, tacked up at a slight hand-placed angle
  • Go to Town as the big pill it already is, bottom-left
  • Currency chips shaped like seed packets, top corners
  • Sage a small sprite by the deck, tip in the card that's already built

Why it's the safe pick

A day or two of work, no new art pipeline, and it still lifts the screen from "prototype" to "product". Ships now; leaves the door open to grow into 1 or 2 later.

Where I'd start

Direction 3 now, direction 1 as the north star. Cosy Flat is a small, safe change that makes the hub feel intentional and buys time. The Windowsill is what Cactus Town should eventually be — a room that reflects your plant, your almanac on the shelf, the town brightening through the glass as your Bloom Score climbs.

The Potting Bench is the compromise if you want a real scene sooner and can spend a week of art on it. All three keep the palette, the plant, and the flat style — the difference is how much of a place you're willing to build.
